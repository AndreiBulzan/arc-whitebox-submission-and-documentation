Date: 2026-08-08

## Question

Can a low-dimensional fixed random projection of each 256-dimensional
complete-basis source vector select and weight a useful sparse basis rule?

For ranks `r in {16,32,64,128}`, eight frozen random orthogonal output
projections, and supports `S in {8,16}`:

1. project all 129 exact basis-source vectors into `r` dimensions;
2. fit the projected total with signed orthogonal matching pursuit;
3. apply the fitted support and weights to the untouched 256-dimensional
   source vectors;
4. repeat on the two packet-replicate halves.

Fit per network and on a four-network concatenated universal sketch.  No
benchmark expectation target is read during selection.

## Gate

A sketched-capacity lead requires at least four of eight fixed sketches at a
given `(r,S)` to achieve, after post-seal scoring:

- at least 35% pooled raw-MSE reduction;
- positive Full and Generated reductions;
- and, for a half-trained fit, positive pooled reduction on the opposite
  packet half.

`r<=32, S=16` is the primary bridge region because it requires only
`2,064--4,128` scalar basis projections.  `r=64,128` diagnose how much output
geometry is needed.  A success is capacity only; the cost of producing exact
basis projections still requires a separate adjoint/contraction derivation.
