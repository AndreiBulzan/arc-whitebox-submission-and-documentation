# Complete packet-pair state depth R3 verdict

Date: 2026-08-07

## Verdict

**Kill routine full-width progressive packet selection and all further linear
GREG work on cheap early states.  Preserve only a separately gated nonlinear
shadow-network research target.**

The exact complete ordered pair state does not clear the frozen capacity gate
until depth 24.  At that point a direct `m=8` progressive implementation has
already paid almost the whole network eight times.  No target, FlopScope
session, physical row, estimator edit, production price, package, upload, or
submission was used.

Depth 24 passed every preregistered component gate.  The depth-32 association
control was exact: direct replay delta and fitted residual were both zero.
The finite-pool association delta was `1.39e-17`, and the conditional random
selection variance was identical at every depth.

The depth-24 `3.4561e-8` residual is below the frozen `4.0e-8` capacity limit,
but slightly above the external follow-up's later public-scale illustrative
allowance of `3.1143e-8` for literal 70% packet-gain retention.  It is therefore
a strong representation result, not a claimed score forecast.

## Compute implication

Propagating all eight candidates through depth `d` and only one candidate
through the remainder costs, before feature/selection overhead,

```text
8d + (32 - d) = 32 + 7d layer-equivalents.
```

At the first passing depth, `d=24`, this is `200` layer-equivalents, or `6.25x`
one complete frame.  This is outside the contest budget by construction.
Earlier progressive pruning does not have a measured selection basis: even
the strongest complete-state linear oracle explains only `25.0%` at depth 4
and `39.4%` at depth 8.

## What remains alive

The result does not refute packet smoothing or one-frame existence:

- the true packet target still has about `53.6%` raw-MSE headroom;
- the finite `m=8` pool retains about `96.1%` of that gain;
- actual-output balancing proves a one-pair-per-line rule exists;
- output deviations remain low-dimensional.

It refutes the proposed cheap bridge.  Useful quenched information is created
through many nonlinear gate interactions.  A surviving bridge must therefore
approximate those interactions directly at low cost—for example, a
target-free width-8/16 actual-weight shadow trained/constructed from lawful
per-network observations.  Such a shadow is not licensed for production.  It
must first clear the same optimal residual gate in a separately preregistered
oracle and must be priced honestly including construction and calibration.
